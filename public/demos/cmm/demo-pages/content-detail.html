Mongolia's Mining Sector: A Comprehensive 2026 Outlook

An in-depth analysis covering copper, gold, coal, and critical minerals — including MSE-listed company performance, foreign investment trends, and regulatory developments shaping the sector through 2027.

NB
Namkhai Batdorj
CMM Research March 28, 2026 14 min read
Mining & Resources Capital Markets

Executive Summary

Mongolia's mining sector enters 2026 on the strongest footing in half a decade. Commodity prices for copper and gold remain elevated amid global supply constraints, and domestic policy reform — particularly the revised Minerals Law enacted in Q4 2025 — has meaningfully improved the regulatory environment for foreign operators. The MSE Mining sub-index has outperformed the broader TOP-20 by 12.3% year-to-date, driven by renewed institutional interest from South Korean and Japanese allocators.

Key companies to watch include Erdene Resource, which posted record quarterly revenue from the Bayan Khundii gold mine, and Oyu Tolgoi, where underground production ramp-up is tracking ahead of the revised feasibility study timeline. Meanwhile, Khan Resources has secured critical licensing for its Dornod uranium deposit, positioning the company as a strategic player in the nuclear energy supply chain. Mongolian Mining Corp continues to benefit from coal logistics improvements along the southern corridor.

"The revised Minerals Law is the single most impactful policy change for Mongolia's resource sector since 2012. It fundamentally realigns the incentive structure for long-term foreign capital."

— Namkhai Batdorj, Head of Research, CMM

We forecast aggregate mining sector market capitalization on the MSE to grow 18-24% by year-end 2026, supported by volume expansion at Oyu Tolgoi, higher coal realizations, and at least two new listings in the critical minerals space. The primary risk vector remains Chinese demand softness, though current data suggests bottoming rather than deterioration.

MSE Mining Performance

The MSE Mining sub-index has been the standout performer in Q1 2026, reaching 1,847 points — a level not seen since the 2012-2013 mining boom. Daily trading volume in mining equities averaged MNT 3.2 billion in March, nearly triple the 2025 daily average. Erdene Resource alone accounted for 22% of total mining volume, reflecting concentrated but high-conviction institutional positioning.

Foreign participation has been a defining feature of this rally. Net foreign inflows into MSE mining equities totaled MNT 48.7 billion in Q1, with South Korean securities firms accounting for approximately 60% of cross-border activity. The CMM Foreign Participation Index for the mining sub-sector reached 0.34 — the highest reading since we began tracking in 2019.

Sector Breakdown

The mining sector's performance is not monolithic — significant divergence exists across commodity subsectors. Copper-gold plays have been the clear winners, benefiting from both price tailwinds and operational milestones. Coal equities have lagged due to persistent uncertainty around Chinese import quotas, though logistics improvements along the Gashuunsukhait-Ganqimaodu corridor have provided modest support. Critical minerals — uranium, lithium, and rare earths — represent the emerging frontier, with early-stage companies attracting speculative interest ahead of anticipated government incentive packages.

Copper remains the dominant value driver, accounting for approximately 45% of total mining sector market capitalization. Oyu Tolgoi's underground block cave is the single largest variable — management has guided for 500,000 tonnes of annual copper production at full capacity, which would make it the world's fourth-largest copper mine. Current underground development is tracking 3-4 months ahead of the revised December 2025 feasibility study, with first sustainable production expected in Q3 2026. The implications for Mongolia's GDP growth, balance of payments position, and MSE listing potential are transformative.

Copper Sub-Sector Analysis

Copper remains the dominant value driver, accounting for approximately 45% of total mining sector market capitalization. Oyu Tolgoi's underground block cave is the single largest variable — management has guided for 500,000 tonnes of annual copper production at full capacity, which would make it the world's fourth-largest copper mine.

Current underground development is tracking 3-4 months ahead of the revised December 2025 feasibility study, with first sustainable production expected in Q3 2026. The implications for Mongolia's GDP growth, balance of payments position, and MSE listing potential are transformative.

Gold Production Outlook

Erdene Resource's Bayan Khundii mine reached commercial production in Q3 2025 and has since ramped to 85% of nameplate capacity. At current gold prices above $2,100/oz, the operation is generating significant free cash flow — management has signaled potential for a maiden dividend in 2026.

The broader Khundii Gold District remains underexplored, with multiple satellite deposits identified within trucking distance of the central processing plant. Exploration drilling results from Altan Nar continue to extend the known mineralization, with grade intercepts consistently above 5 g/t Au.

Regulatory Environment

The Mongolian government's revised mining royalty framework, effective January 2026, introduces a sliding scale tied to commodity prices. At current copper prices, effective royalty rates fall between 5-7%, competitive with Chile and Peru. The framework provides much-needed predictability for foreign investors who had previously cited regulatory uncertainty as the primary barrier to allocation.

Investment Recommendations

For institutional investors seeking Mongolian mining exposure, we recommend a barbell approach: core allocation to liquid MSE-listed names (Erdene Resource, Mongolian Mining Corp) complemented by selective exposure to pre-production projects with clear catalysts. The risk-reward profile favors companies with operational assets generating cash flow over exploration-stage juniors.
